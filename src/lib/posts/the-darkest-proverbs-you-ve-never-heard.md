## "The child who is not embraced by the village will burn it down to feel its warmth." - African proverb

> Seres humanos abandonados nutrirão desejos destrutivos para se aproximarem de outros humanos.

<details>
<summary>Show analysis</summary>

I understand this proverb as being about the destructive consequences of rejection. Someone who is denied affection, belonging, and acceptance may eventually become destructive precisely because they desire those things. The destruction itself becomes a twisted attempt to approach the community that rejected them.

### Analytical perspective

The interpretation goes beyond the metaphor in one important way. "Destructive desires" may not necessarily be what the proverb describes. The person may still desire belonging and warmth, while destructive behavior becomes their distorted way of pursuing it.

In other words, rejection may not create a desire for destruction. It may turn the desire for connection into destructive action.

> Seres humanos abandonados podem se tornar destrutivos justamente porque ainda desejam pertencer.

</details>

## "If you seek revenge, dig two graves." - Chinese proverb

> Ao buscar vingança, lembre-se que sua vida estará em jogo também.

<details>
<summary>Show analysis</summary>

I understand this proverb as a warning that revenge is never completely one-sided. If you decide to harm someone because they harmed you, you are also putting yourself in danger.

The second grave represents the possibility that the person seeking revenge will eventually suffer the same fate as the person they want to punish.

### Analytical perspective

The interpretation is straightforward, but perhaps too literal. The "second grave" does not necessarily represent physical death.

Seeking revenge can also destroy someone psychologically, morally, socially, or emotionally. A person may succeed in hurting their enemy and still lose something of themselves in the process.

The proverb may be deeper than the interpretation suggests: revenge does not necessarily have to kill you to bury you.

> Ao buscar vingança, lembre-se de que ela também poderá destruir você.

</details>

## "When elephants fight, it is the grass that suffers." - African proverb

> Quando potências estão em conflitos, aqueles sob seus cuidados sofrerão.

<details>
<summary>Show analysis</summary>

I interpret this as a statement about the consequences of conflicts between powerful forces. When powers fight each other, those who depend on them can end up suffering the consequences.

The grass does not participate in the elephants' fight, yet it is the thing being crushed beneath them.

### Analytical perspective

The interpretation is close to the central metaphor, although the phrase "aqueles sob seus cuidados" narrows its scope.

The grass is not necessarily under the elephants' care. It is simply weaker and unable to escape the consequences of their conflict.

That distinction makes the proverb broader. It can refer not only to people under the responsibility of powerful groups, but to anyone who is caught between forces much stronger than themselves.

> Quando potências entram em conflito, aqueles que estão abaixo delas podem sofrer sem sequer fazer parte da disputa.

</details>

## "The snow that falls on the grave does not warm the living." - Japanese proverb

> Compartilhe momentos bons com aqueles que você ama, antes que o destino os leve embora.

<details>
<summary>Show analysis</summary>

I interpret this proverb as a reminder that there are things that cannot be recovered after someone dies.

We can remember someone, mourn them, preserve their belongings, and keep their memory alive. But none of these things can replace the moments we could have shared while they were still alive.

The snow falling on the grave cannot warm the living. In the same way, regret after someone's death cannot replace the affection and experiences that could have existed before it.

### Analytical perspective

The interpretation turns the proverb into direct advice: spend good moments with the people you love before you lose them.

The original metaphor is somewhat more restrained. It focuses on the limitations of what remains after death rather than explicitly telling us how to live.

Still, the interpretation follows naturally from the image. If the "warmth" represents affection, companionship, and human connection, then it makes sense to argue that these things should be given while they can still be experienced.

> Valorize aqueles que você ama enquanto estão vivos, pois o afeto oferecido depois da morte já não pode aquecer os vivos.

</details>

## "Better to see something once than hear about it a thousand times." - Mongolian proverb

> Siga os seus sonhos e veja com seus olhos os seus frutos, ao invés de repetir e ouvir o que você espera conquistar.

<details>
<summary>Show analysis</summary>

Here, I interpret the proverb in a more motivational way.

Hearing about something repeatedly is not the same as experiencing it. If there is something you want to accomplish, there is a point at which you need to stop hearing about what it could be like and experience it yourself.

For me, seeing the results of your own efforts is more meaningful than endlessly hearing about what you might accomplish.

### Analytical perspective

This is the interpretation that departs the most from the apparent meaning of the proverb.

The original idea is more directly about firsthand experience being superior to hearsay. Seeing something once can give you knowledge that hearing someone describe it a thousand times cannot provide.

The interpretation adds ideas of dreams, ambition, and achievement. Those ideas are not necessarily present in the proverb itself.

> A experiência direta vale mais do que inúmeras descrições daquilo que você ainda não vivenciou.

</details>
